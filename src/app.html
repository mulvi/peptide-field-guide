      Safety & sources

      Read this before acting on anything in the guide.

      This is education, not medical advice. It cannot tell you whether a medicine is right for you. Decisions about starting, stopping or dosing any medicine belong with a prescriber who knows your history.

      Research chemicals and compounded peptides

      Many peptides sold online are labelled "research use only" or "not for human consumption". They are not approved medicines. Their purity, dose accuracy and sterility are unverified, and testing of such products has found mislabelled and contaminated samples.

      The US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) has raised safety concerns about several peptides used in compounding, including BPC-157, and has at times restricted their use by compounding pharmacies. These rules change, so check the current FDA position. An approved medicine from a licensed pharmacy is a different thing from a vial bought online, even when the name is the same.

      Injection basics

      • Learn injection technique from a pharmacist, nurse or prescriber, not from a video alone.
      • Use new sterile needles and syringes every time, and clean the vial stopper before each use.
      • Never share needles, syringes or vials with anyone.
      • Put used sharps straight into a sharps container and ask your pharmacy how to dispose of it locally.
      • Check the syringe type matches your calculation. This guide assumes U-100 insulin syringes, where 1 mL equals 100 units.

      When to seek urgent care

      • Swelling of the face, lips or throat, trouble breathing, or a widespread rash after a dose.
      • Severe stomach pain that does not go away, with or without vomiting, which can be a sign of pancreatitis.
      • Chest pain, fainting, a very fast heartbeat, or signs of very low blood sugar such as confusion or shaking.
      • An injection site that becomes hot, red, swollen and painful, or fever after injecting.
      • An erection lasting more than four hours.

      If in doubt, contact emergency services or your local urgent care service.

      Dosing guide

      How dosing schedules work, what approved labels say, and what published trials used.

      This page reports. It does not recommend. Label schedules are summaries of FDA prescribing information. Trial doses are what researchers gave under supervision. Neither tells you what you should take.

      How schedules work

      • Doses step up slowly. Starting low and raising the dose every few weeks gives the body time to adjust and reduces nausea and other side effects. Labels set a minimum time on each step, usually 4 weeks.
      • Half-life sets the frequency. Semaglutide stays in the body for about a week and tirzepatide for about 5 days, so both are weekly. Liraglutide lasts about 13 hours, so it is daily.
      • Levels build for weeks. A weekly medicine takes about 4 to 5 weeks to reach a steady level. That is why raising the dose early adds side effects without adding benefit.
      • Some doses depend on body weight. A dose written as mcg per kg is multiplied by your weight. For example, 30 mcg per kg at 80 kg is 2,400 mcg, which is 2.4 mg.
      • Units are volume. 1 mg is 1,000 mcg. Syringe units measure liquid (100 units is 1 mL), so the same number of units can hold very different amounts of peptide. The Calculator converts between them.
      • A range is a ceiling and a floor. Labels aim for the lowest dose that works and is tolerated. Side effects rise with dose.
      • Never double up. No label tells you to take two doses to make up for a missed one.
      • Cycling is untested. Patterns such as weeks on and weeks off are common online for unapproved peptides. No controlled trial has tested them.

      Approved medicines: labelled schedules

      Semaglutide Wegovy, weight management
      Range
      0.25 mg to 2.4 mg, once weekly
      Schedule
      0.25 mg for 4 weeks, then 0.5 mg, 1 mg and 1.7 mg for 4 weeks each, then 2.4 mg. Some people stay on 1.7 mg.
      Missed dose
      Take it if the next dose is more than 2 days away. Otherwise skip it. After more than 2 missed doses in a row, ask your prescriber whether to restart the steps.
      Semaglutide Ozempic, type 2 diabetes
      Range
      0.25 mg to 2 mg, once weekly
      Schedule
      0.25 mg for 4 weeks, then 0.5 mg. Increases to 1 mg and then 2 mg come after at least 4 weeks on the current dose, only if needed.
      Missed dose
      Take it within 5 days of the missed day. After that, skip it.
      Tirzepatide Mounjaro, Zepbound
      Range
      2.5 mg to 15 mg, once weekly
      Schedule
      2.5 mg for 4 weeks (a starting dose only), then 5 mg. Increases are 2.5 mg at a time, after at least 4 weeks on the current dose. Maintenance is 5 mg, 10 mg or 15 mg.
      Missed dose
      Take it within 4 days of the missed day. After that, skip it.
      Liraglutide Saxenda, weight management
      Range
      0.6 mg to 3 mg, once daily
      Schedule
      0.6 mg for 1 week, then up by 0.6 mg each week (1.2, 1.8, 2.4) to 3 mg.
      Missed dose
      Skip it and take the next dose as usual. Never double up. After more than 3 days without a dose, the label says to restart at 0.6 mg.
      Liraglutide Victoza, type 2 diabetes
      Range
      0.6 mg to 1.8 mg, once daily
      Schedule
      0.6 mg for 1 week, then 1.2 mg. An increase to 1.8 mg is possible if needed.
      Missed dose
      Same rule as Saxenda.
      Tesamorelin Egrifta SV
      Dose
      1.4 mg once daily, fixed. There are no steps.
      Notes
      Approved only for excess abdominal fat in HIV lipodystrophy. Other Egrifta formulations use different amounts, so follow the label for your product.
      Bremelanotide Vyleesi
      Dose
      1.75 mg as needed, at least 45 minutes before sexual activity.
      Limits
      No more than 1 dose in 24 hours and no more than 8 doses a month. The label says to stop after 8 weeks without improvement.
      Setmelanotide Imcivree
      Schedule
      For adults the label starts at 2 mg once daily for 2 weeks, then 3 mg once daily if tolerated. Children start lower.
      Notes
      Only for specific rare genetic forms of obesity, confirmed by testing and managed by a specialist.

      Approved outside the US

      Thymosin alpha-1 Zadaxin, approved in some countries
      Label dose
      1.6 mg under the skin twice a week, for 6 months, in the hepatitis B indication.
      Notes
      Not FDA-approved. Other uses seen online have no labelled dose.
      Semax and Selank approved in Russia
      Form
      Nasal drops, given in short courses.
      Notes
      Dosing follows the Russian product label, which this guide does not reproduce. Neither is approved in the US, UK or EU.

      Doses used in published trials

      These are records of what researchers tested. They are not recommendations, and several of these trials failed.

      Retatrutide phase 2 obesity trial, 2023
      Doses tested
      1 mg, 4 mg, 8 mg or 12 mg once weekly for 48 weeks.
      How
      The higher doses were reached in steps, starting at 2 mg or 4 mg.
      Status
      Investigational. There is no approved product and no label.
      Sermorelin former Geref label
      Doses tested
      30 mcg per kg of body weight, once daily at bedtime, in children with growth hormone deficiency.
      Status
      That product is no longer sold. Doses from compounding pharmacies are set by the prescriber.
      CJC-1295 small healthy-volunteer studies, 2006
      Doses tested
      Weight-based, about 30 to 60 mcg per kg, given weekly or every two weeks.
      Status
      Development stopped. No trial showed a health benefit, so no dose is established.
      Ipamorelin phase 2 trial after bowel surgery
      Doses tested
      0.03 mg per kg into a vein, twice daily for up to 7 days, in hospital.
      Status
      The trial showed no benefit. The route and setting differ from how it is sold online.
      AOD-9604 obesity trials
      Doses tested
      Daily oral tablets, mostly 1 mg a day or less in the largest trial.
      Status
      The trial did not show meaningful weight loss. The injected form sold online is not what the main trials tested.
      Collagen peptides oral supplement trials
      Doses tested
      2.5 g to 10 g a day by mouth, usually for 8 to 24 weeks.
      Status
      A food supplement. Products vary, so follow the product label.

      No established human dose

      For BPC-157, TB-500, MOTS-c, epitalon, melanotan II and injectable GHK-Cu, no controlled human trial has established a dose, a schedule or a safe range.
